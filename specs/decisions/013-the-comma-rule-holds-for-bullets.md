# 013. The comma rule holds for bullets, and PHILOSOPHY argues in prose with any structure

Decided: 2026-10-31 and 2026-11-01

## The problem

Paragraphs that held separate points were hard to scan, so a bullet was made one sentence with at most one comma. SKILL.md later wrote the rule for every sentence, which held explanatory prose to a rule meant for bullets. The PHILOSOPHY guide also said the doc is prose only, though some of its points are separate and read better as a list or a table.

## The choice

A bullet is one sentence and keeps at most one comma, while a paragraph is not held to a comma count. PHILOSOPHY argues in prose and uses a list, a table or another structure wherever it shows the thought better.

## Not chosen

- One comma in every sentence: prose that argues needs longer sentences, and the rule would chop it into fragments.
- An exception for PHILOSOPHY alone: other docs explain in paragraphs too, and the rule was never about paragraphs.
- PHILOSOPHY as prose only: separate failures packed into a paragraph read as a run of sentences with no shape.

## Consequences

Paragraphs in every doc may use the sentence length the argument needs. Some sentences written in the meantime were split to meet the wider rule, and they can stay as they are.
